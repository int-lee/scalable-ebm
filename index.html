Abstractclick to expand

Energy-Based Models (EBMs) are a flexible class of generative models that decouple probabilistic modeling from the generation process. This decoupling directly enables compositional generation and inference-time scaling, where sample quality can be refined simply by increasing sampling steps. However, severe challenges in high-dimensional sampling and training instabilities with deep architectures have restricted EBMs to low-dimensional regimes.

To resolve these bottlenecks, we introduce the Transformer Energy-Based Model (TEM), a scalable transformer energy function operating entirely within a latent space. On top of this backbone, we extract energies at multiple layers and sum them, composing complementary constraints from local texture to global semantics. Furthermore, two components make this high-quality at scale: Positive Representation Alignment (PRA), which supervises TEM's intermediate representations with a vision foundation model, and advanced sampling strategies that improve sample quality at inference.

TEM-XL achieves an FID-50K of 3.82 on class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 without classifier-free guidance, showing that a contrastive EBM can generate high-fidelity images. On ImageNet 512×512, TEM achieves an FID-50K of 8.24, demonstrating that the approach scales to higher resolution. We further validate the core promises of EBMs: compositional generation, zero-shot inpainting, and out-of-distribution detection.

How TEM trains and samples. During training, MCMC samples $x^-$ are drawn with Langevin dynamics and the energy is pushed down on real data $x^+$ and up on $x^-$. At inference, Langevin sampling on the trained energy descends the landscape and lands on the real data.

Starting from another class’s image

↔ Drag a slider: the chain starts from an image of A and moves on B’s energy landscape only.

Each chain starts from an image of the source class (step 0) and runs 80 Langevin steps following only the target class’s energy gradient. The source energy (dashed) is never optimized, only measured: it rises purely as a side effect as the image leaves the source basin and settles into the target one.

Compositional Generation

+

Composition is just addition: the summed energy is lowest where both concepts are satisfied, so sampling on $E_1 + E_2$ lands on an image containing both.

For two energy-defined distributions, the product $p_1(x)p_2(x) \propto \exp(-(E_1(x)+E_2(x)))$. Sampling with the summed energy gives images that satisfy both concepts at once.

Out-of-Distribution Detection

An EBM scores inputs directly: in-distribution data sits in the low-energy basin, while unfamiliar inputs land at high energy. A single forward pass of the unconditional energy $E_\theta(x)$ gives an OOD score, with no task-specific training, labels, or calibration. Diffusion baselines are scored by their denoising residual with one network evaluation. SiT+REPA receives the same DINOv2 alignment yet scores close to SiT, so alignment alone does not explain the gap: the scalar energy does.

Zero-Shot Inpainting

With an energy-based model, inpainting needs no retraining. We start sampling directly on the masked area, following the energy gradient only there while the rest of the image stays fixed, so the missing part fills in to match its surroundings.

Method

Multi-layer Energy Composition

$\sum$

Multi-layer energy composition. TEM processes VAE-encoded latent tokens through a stack of TEM blocks, and energy heads at early, middle and final depths each output a scalar energy. Sampling from one head at a time shows what each one enforces: the early head recovers local texture without global structure, the middle head captures object layout without fine detail, and the final head keeps only coarse semantics. Sampling from their sum satisfies all three at once.

Positive Representation Alignment

Left: FID-50K over training for TEM-B; the buttons step through the three settings and update the TEM diagram. PRA lowers FID from 42.8 to 19.9 at 50K steps, while also aligning negatives (Pos+Neg RA) reaches only 36.6. Right: switch between TEM and diffusion. Both align mid-layer features with a frozen VFM. Diffusion (REPA) aligns features of a noisy $x_t$ to the clean $x_0$ target, guiding denoising. TEM aligns only dataset images $x^+$, where both networks see the same clean input; Langevin samples $x^-$ get no alignment, since they lie off the data manifold and aligning them would work against the contrastive push.
